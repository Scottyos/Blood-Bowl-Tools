How chain pushes actually work (quick reference)

Picking a push square

When your block resolves as a Push, you don't get to send the defender anywhere you like. Draw a line from your attacker through the defender — the defender can only go into one of the three squares directly on the far side of that line (the square dead ahead, plus the two beside it). That's why this trainer highlights exactly three candidate squares per push.

Chain pushing

  1. If your chosen square is empty, the defender lands there and the chain stops.
  2. If your chosen square is occupied — by anyone, either team — that player gets shoved too, using the same three-square logic, now measured from the square your first mover just came from.
  3. If a push square is off the pitch, that player is surfed into the crowd — an automatic injury, no armour roll needed.

Frenzy

An attacker with Frenzy must follow up into the square the defender just vacated after any push. If the defender is still standing and still adjacent, the attacker must block them again — a second Push, resolved the same way. That second block is often the difference between "shoved once" and "gone."
